## Findings

1. **Stripe callbacks cannot account for all paid events** (cascade)
   - Evidence: From 11:18 through 11:36 UTC, `metrics.json` shows 447 `payment.succeeded` messages and 439 Stripe webhooks even after counting the 11:17 webhooks. The generator publishes one success message per successful webhook within about a second. The incident's per-minute check can use the same previous-minute callbacks twice. Over the 45-minute outage, Stripe requests fall by 462 and the Payment HTTP series removes about 924 requests, but Stripe webhooks fall by 454 after the count adjustments.
   - Fix: Generate and count the callback, Payment and Gateway requests, and Kafka events from the same successful checkout records. Apply the actual event delays before counting by minute.

2. **Stock-hold expiry still reports fast releases** (cascade)
   - Evidence: At 11:39:10.632 UTC, `logs.jsonl` reports six expired stock holds released in 33 ms. `seed/noise.py` keeps the expiry job at baseline speed and gives no indication of what it writes. The topology includes stock release, and the checkout release path in `seed/generate.py` updates the same `stock` table as reservation. The job's behavior under the invalid index and row locks is unaccounted for.
   - Fix: Show which write path the expiry job uses. If it updates the affected rows, model the added latency and any contention or skipped rows. If it uses a separate path, make that visible in the telemetry.

3. **Resampling changes an independent payment result** (cascade)
   - Evidence: Baseline order `ord_E4ZZAQ` at 07:00:35.198 UTC is `PAYMENT_FAILED` and releases its stock. The scenario replaces it at the same timestamp with `ord_YURXY9`, which pays and ships. The incident reruns all post-migration checkout samples without preserving their decline outcome, while the metric model keeps the underlying declines. The incident consequently has no sampled post-migration payment decline or stock release.
   - Fix: Preserve unaffected outcomes when rebuilding the samples and slow the surviving release spans. Replace a baseline attempt only when the incident changes its outcome.

4. **Expected and reference impact values disagree** (fact)
   - Evidence: `business_kpis` and the good fixture's tool result report 62.14% checkout success and a 24.14% worst minute at 11:56 UTC. The fixture's answer and `outcome/prompt.md` use 62.3% and 21.4% or about 21%. The logs contain 462 sustained Catalog timeout errors and 462 matching Order errors. The stock failure metric totals 467, including five baseline failures, and 180 of 292 attempts in the last ten minutes. Both prompts instead use 460 and 465, and the outcome prompt rounds the last ten minutes to 61%. The label's detailed impact matches the data.
   - Fix: Use 62.14%, 24.14%, 462 timeouts, 467 total reservation failures, and about 62% for the last ten minutes. Generate reference numbers from the same data as the tools.

5. **Some scenario claims are estimates or need narrower wording** (fact)
   - Evidence: `labels.json` gives the pre-break sync speed as 3.2 ms per row. The start-only cron logs cannot establish that number or the old-speed runtime in the trajectory prompt. The value comes from `seed/incidents.py`. The first isolated timeout is at 10:15 UTC on PID 9296, order `ord_Y2ZNC2`. Payment success runs as low as 84.61% in individual fault minutes despite having no sustained alert. This qualifies the label's statements that failures start at 11:15 and payment success stays normal.
   - Fix: Mark the two-minute old-speed estimate as a counterfactual based on the seed. Describe 11:15 as the start of the sustained outage. Describe the Payment evidence as normal provider and service health with short dips in the KPI.

6. **Impact credit depends on the call and window more than the result** (false negative)
   - Evidence: `checked_checkout_impact` gives 0.5 for a healthy 06:00–06:30 KPI result. It gives only 0.5 for the real 78.567% checkout-success point at 11:19, and zero for a direct search returning six timeout logs by 11:19. Full credit for the log path uses a count or a pattern match without checking for timeout or failure. A future KPI call with a null success result correctly scores zero.
   - Fix: Require measured degradation or incident-specific failures. Accept real early-window points and counts. Match failure logs by service, status, and error.

7. **The trace checks can credit the wrong run or an unverified link** (false negative)
   - Evidence: The 10:15 trace with PID 9296 earns full `found_lock_wait_timeout` and `checked_failure_before_payment` even when paired only with cron logs for PID 5089. Those results do not explain the paged run. `matched_lock_holder` earns 1 when the 11:23 trace is paired with only the 11:15 start line. Its comment also calls for progress after the failed request. A real `logs_by_trace` result plus checkout trace summaries earns 1 for the same-request timeout and zero for the pre-Payment boundary, even though the summaries list the participating services and the joined logs identify the Catalog failure.
   - Fix: Scope full credit to a Catalog stock failure during the paged run and tie the boundary to the same trace or order. Use subsequent progress or a transaction-state result when the investigation claims the run remains open as of noon. Allow the same-trace log and summary combination.

8. **The slowdown join can be too loose or reject valid comparisons** (false negative)
   - Evidence: Catalog p95 points at 06:12–06:13 and 07:09–07:10 plus the failed DDL score 1 for both `found_slowdown_at_0641` and `matched_build_to_slowdown`. They bracket nearly an hour. A real `compare_windows` call for 06:35–06:41 and 06:41–06:50 scores zero for both because the inclusive tool window puts the 78.60 ms point in the before maximum. The adjacent 06:40 and 06:41 points do pass. `found_failed_index_build` scores 1 from the failed build log alone. Its predicate does not require migration 0142, a CREATE step, or the successful old-index drop.
   - Fix: Define the window boundaries consistently and locate the step with adjacent points or tightly bounded comparisons. Bind the measured change to the migration's timestamp. Distinguish the failed build from evidence of the complete migration sequence.

9. **Efficiency reaches full credit with repeated or unrelated calls** (false positive)
   - Evidence: A five-call investigation that proves the evidence links keeps `efficiency=1` after the same KPI call is added twice. It also keeps 1 after an extra alert-list call. A useful path beyond ten effective calls starts losing credit regardless of what those calls establish. The trajectory prompt grades contribution and repeated calls.
   - Fix: Separate useful evidence, unrelated calls, and duplicates. Apply the same criteria to short and long investigations.

10. **A wrong final answer earns all eight deterministic outcome points** (false positive)
    - Evidence: With no tool calls, this answer scored 5/5 for outcome. "Migration 0142 fixed an invalid stock index. The restock took 50 minutes because it was large. The sole culprit: newsletter. The sync locks a mutex. Checkout returned 503. Cancel all sync sessions. Rebuild the stock index using a blocking build. Between 11:15 and 12:00, 50% of checkouts succeeded. Before payment we emailed everyone. Checkout latency is still improving." Separately, saying `stock_sku_warehouse_idx` has `indisvalid=true` earns `names_invalid_index=1`. A bare invalid-index claim earns `no_false_lead_as_cause=1`.
    - Fix: Grade affirmed facts and linked causal claims. Check index validity, the direction of the migration's effect, why stock writes slowed, and which transaction blocked the timed-out reservation. Scope mitigation and repair to Catalog's incident. Validate the rate or count against its measure and window, and require the stated no-charge and ongoing-lock consequences.

11. **Equivalent diagnoses and actions lose outcome credit** (false negative)
    - Evidence: A correct answer with the nine good tool results scored 3.13/5 for outcome. It described the lost access path and seventy-millisecond row writes across sentences, asked to "End the warehouse_stock_sync transaction for 5089," and said failed reservations "created zero payment intents" and the sync "had yet to commit." It lost `explains_slow_sync`, `gives_mitigation`, and `gives_impact_and_status`. Other probes gave zero for the affected items when a diagnosis described the two-second deadline and "Service Unavailable," named the 06:41 Catalog DDL without the word "migration," or said "Recreate it concurrently" after identifying the index. Calling the restock the cause of the lock waits while naming the broken index as the upstream cause lost `no_false_lead_as_cause`.
    - Fix: Accept equivalent descriptions across sentences. Resolve references to the job, backend, and index. Distinguish immediate lock contention from the root cause.

12. **Mitigation and repair can score without a valid plan** (coverage)
    - Evidence: The bad fixture's `pg_cancel_backend(5089)` with no rollback check earns `gives_mitigation=1`. "Backend 5089 was terminated" also gets full credit as an action. The outcome tests accept a blocking index rebuild and omit confirmation of lock release, checkout recovery, and index validity or update latency before the retry. The outcome prompt requires rollback verification if using cancellation.
    - Fix: Require a proposed action that ends the blocking transaction or verifies cancellation led to rollback. Grade verification of lock release, checkout recovery, and index repair before the restock resumes. [PostgreSQL server signaling](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/functions-admin.html#FUNCTIONS-ADMIN-SIGNAL) and [concurrent index repair](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/17/sql-createindex.html).

13. **The trajectory prompt has conflicting or underspecified anchors** (coverage)
    - Evidence: `evidence_links` accepts any order, while `widened_the_time_window` can give half credit for finding the migration first and confirming the earlier metric step later. Its "looks back" anchor can also give half credit to a long query that finds nothing useful. `course_correction` lists some alternatives in its anchor and more in its evidence. The good fixture retrieves the DLQ alert and leaves it unexplained. It is unclear whether that alone counts as encountering a plausible cause. The deterministic checks score the good tool results with a blank answer at 5/5 for trajectory, so they do not establish whether the agent explained the evidence or ruled out a lead.
    - Fix: Score evidence of the earlier step independently of discovery order. Define which leads warrant a response and what qualifies as evidence. Clarify how the tool-result score relates to the score for the agent's reasoning.

14. **Some outcome prompt anchors overlap and some evidence goes ungraded** (coverage)
    - Evidence: `root_cause` has no clear partial anchor for a correct migration and concurrent failure with the timestamp omitted. `mechanism` can withhold full credit if matching evidence establishes the PID but the prose omits the number. `impact` can award half for a correct rate alongside a wrong charge claim. `next_steps` specifies recovery checks, but the full anchor says "cancel" without repeating the conditional rollback check in the expected facts. `evidence` gives half for some unsupported claims and zero for facts the transcript does not show. The deterministic grader has no citation check. Neither prompt explicitly scores the lost paid order, emails, or booking, even though the failed order timeline establishes those consequences.
    - Fix: Set partial anchors for missing time or identifiers, false charge claims, and unsupported versus contradicted evidence. Align the action anchors with transaction rollback and verification. Ask for citations and score the downstream customer effects if they are part of the required impact.
